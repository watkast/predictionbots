# Missing protective layers in industries people are afraid to enter (2026-10-17)

Owner: solo US operator (Colorado), Claude Code agents, $1k–$20k. Thesis to test: **specialize in building the boring business/compliance/safety layer that is missing in stigmatized or fragmented legal markets** — markets where legal participants are being hurt (economically or physically) because a feed, registry, verification, workflow or data standard does not exist, and incumbents will not build it because of stigma, fragmentation, payment-processor fear or fast-moving rules. Archetype: `av_compliance_feed` in `taboo_legal.md` §1d/§8 (age-verification rules matrix for small platforms).

Hard lines (unchanged): US-legal only; nothing that exploits participants; the layer must protect or empower the people in the market, not extract from their vulnerability. Corollary we apply here: **if the only payer is the vulnerable party and the price would be a tax on their vulnerability, the lane is grant/nonprofit, not a venture.**

Grades: **A** statute/regulator/court/peer-reviewed or large-sample · **B** practitioner or industry data with method · **C** vendor/marketing · **D** anecdote or modelled. Sub-scores 1–5: **H** harm addressed (size × severity), **W** willingness-to-pay of an identifiable buyer, **A** automatability by agents; **R** legal + processor + platform risk 1 low / 3 high. **Score = H×W×A ÷ R.** Bar for `configs/ventures.yaml`: score ≥ 25 and a buyer who is *not* the vulnerable party.

## 1. Adult / creator economy

**Participants:** 4.6M creator accounts and 377.5M fan accounts on OnlyFans alone (FY2024, [RTÉ](https://rte.ie/news/business/2025/0822/1529677-onlyfans-results)) [A]; thousands of small independent sites, forums, cam and dating platforms.

### 1a. TAKE IT DOWN Act — the real tooling gap (→ #2)
* Law: notice-and-removal obligations effective **May 19, 2026**; covered platforms must remove nonconsensual intimate imagery (incl. AI deepfakes) **within 48 hours** of a valid request and make reasonable efforts to remove identical copies; civil penalties **up to $53,088 per violation**; FTC sent letters to >12 companies before the deadline, launched TakeItDown.ftc.gov for complaints; DOJ has its first conviction and a domain seizure ([WilmerHale](https://wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260615-the-take-it-down-act-goes-live)) [A]; FTC stakeholder letter ([FTC PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/TIDA-Stakeholder-Letter.pdf)) [A].
* Covered platform = any public site that "primarily provides a forum for user-generated content" (social, forums, game sites) or regularly hosts intimate depictions; **no small-business exemption**; counsel advises small sites to "assume in scope until you have a written analysis" ([beancount small-business guide](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/08/13/take-it-down-act-48-hour-takedown-small-business-compliance-guide), [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102ms5e/covered-platforms-are-you-ready-to-take-it-down/)) [B].
* Required mechanics: public intake channel that works without login, separate queue, evidence preservation (hash, URL, uploader, timestamp), 48-h clock incl. weekends, identical-copy search (PhotoDNA/PDQ/perceptual hash), requester + uploader notice, **2-year record retention**, quarterly repeat-upload review ([beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/08/13/take-it-down-act-48-hour-takedown-small-business-compliance-guide)) [B].
* Why missing: enterprise T&S stacks (Hive, Thorn Safer, Microsoft PhotoDNA, StopNCII partner program) are priced and gated for large platforms; the SMB guides name **no vendor at all** and tell owners to staff two people. Forum/dating/adult/gaming SMBs are exactly the customers Stripe-era SaaS vendors avoid.
* The layer: hosted intake page + embeddable widget; ticket queue with SLA timer; PDQ/pHash index of the platform's own media (hash only, never store the image); duplicate scan + removal API hooks for common stacks (Discourse, XenForo, phpBB, WordPress/bbPress, custom via REST); notices; immutable 2-year log + quarterly report; written "covered-platform analysis" template. Pair with `av_compliance_feed` as one "small-platform compliance kit".
* Who pays: platform operators, **$29–99/mo** (anchor: SMB compliance SaaS; a single violation is $53k). Non-adult forums are the volume; adult/creator sites the urgency.
* Legal/licensing: no licence; **never retain the reported imagery** (hash + metadata only) — storing NCII creates its own exposure; DMCA-agent style ToS; ADA-accessible form. Processor: customers include adult sites → bill through Paddle/Lemon Squeezy (merchant-of-record) and keep CCBill as a fallback; the kit itself is not adult content.
* Agents: build and host (Workers + D1/R2 for hashes), maintain integrations, draft outreach; humans: nothing in the loop except support.
* Time-to-first-dollar: 3–6 weeks (law already in force; FTC letters create urgency). Defensibility: low on tech, medium on integrations + audit-log trust + bundling with AV feed.
* 20-contact demand test: 8 XenForo/Discourse forum owners (hobby, gaming, fitness), 4 small dating/creator platforms (not OnlyFans), 4 indie adult sites via XBIZ/ YNOT forums, 2 WordPress community-plugin authors, 2 T&S consultants. Ask: "how do you handle a TIDA notice today, and what would you pay to have it handled in 48 h with a log?" Pass = 5 of 20 say they would pay ≥$29/mo or ask for a demo.

## 2. Cannabis / hemp

### 2a. Hemp-THC federal redefinition (→ #1)
* Timeline: the Nov-2025 federal law redefined hemp; **H.R. 6500 (signed Sep 2, 2026)** split the cliff: **Nov 12, 2026** ban on products with cannabinoids not naturally produced by *Cannabis sativa* (synthetics); **Dec 11, 2026** total-THC standard (0.3 % dry weight incl. THCA) and **0.4 mg total THC per container** cap for final-form products; states vary (RI adopts federal definition Dec 11 with no transition; NJ beverage transition to Nov 13, 2026) ([Vicente LLP](https://vicentellp.com/insights/federal-hemp-definition-change-delay/)) [A/B]; earlier analysis [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2025/11/new-federal-restrictions-on-hemp-and-hemp-derived-products), [Foley Hoag](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/cannabis-and-the-law/2026/september/a-temporary-reprieve-for-hemp-thc-products…-but-what-now/), state guidance e.g. [MN OCM](https://mn.gov/ocm/businesses/resources/hemp/federal-hemp-changes.jsp) [A].
* Participants and harm: hemp-cannabinoid TAM **$38.7B**, ~**50,000 operators**, ~**350,000 jobs**; the survey projects **$28.3B** retail revenue loss, **~226k** jobs displaced, >70 % of businesses closing or relocating, only **8.5 %** of current products remaining saleable ([Whitney Economics 2026 via BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260909671645/en/Whitney-Economics-Releases-U.S.-Hemp-Cannabinoid-Report-for-2026)) [B, industry-commissioned survey]; c-store category at risk ([NACS](https://convenience.org/stay-current/news/2026/january/19/1-hemp-thc-ban-could-kill-a-burgeoning-category_gr)) [B].
* Why missing: cannabis compliance SaaS (Metrc integrators, Simplifya, Flourish) is built for *licensed* cannabis and does not touch hemp; law firms publish alerts, not machine-readable rules; rules changed twice in 10 months; CBD is "restricted" and >limit THC is "marijuana/prohibited" at Stripe ([Stripe restricted businesses](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses)) [A], so SaaS vendors avoid the customer list.
* The layer: (i) `data/hemp_rules.json` — per state: adopts federal definition? date, transition window, total-THC test method, container cap, beverage carve-outs, intrastate frameworks (MN-style), enforcement agency, penalty; (ii) **COA checker**: upload a lab COA → total-THC (Δ9 + 0.877·THCA + listed analogues) per serving and per container vs 0.4 mg cap and 0.3 % dry-weight → go / reformulate / withdraw by state; (iii) weekly diff feed + email; (iv) later: licensed-channel pivot directory (state cannabis license costs/timelines).
* Who pays: brands/manufacturers ($99–299/mo during transition, then $49), distributors and c-store chains (data licence $2–10k/yr), trade associations (sponsor the free state map), law firms (white-label). Time-to-first-dollar: **2–4 weeks** — the deadline is 4–8 weeks away.
* Legal: informational, "not legal advice", lawyer review of the matrix (budget $1–2k); no licence needed; we handle no product.
* Agents: scrape/maintain statutes + state agency pages; COA parsing (PDF → table) is squarely agent work; weekly diff bot. Defensibility: low after the cliff; medium if it becomes the standing "fringe consumables regulatory feed" (hemp + **kratom/7-OH**: ≥31 states + DC regulate kratom, 7 schedule it, TN bans it, FDA recommended Schedule I for 7-OH, 19 states label rules, 22 age limits ([NCSL Jun-2026](https://www.ncsl.org/resources/details/lawmakers-weigh-guardrails-bans-on-kratom-and-7-oh)) [A]; kava; nicotine pouches).
* 20-contact test: 6 hemp-beverage brands (list from NACS/Hemp Beverage Alliance), 4 gummy/edible manufacturers, 4 CBD/hemp retailers in CO/TX/FL/MN, 2 hemp distributors, 2 trade groups (US Hemp Roundtable, Hemp Beverage Alliance), 2 cannabis law firms. Offer the free state map, ask for $149 for the COA checker. Pass = 4 paid or 8 "send me the map weekly".

* Tax (→ #4): Kalshi issues 1099-MISC only for ≥$600 bonuses and 1099-INT for interest; contract P&L arrives on **no form**; Polymarket issues nothing; no IRS guidance on characterization (other income vs §165(d) gambling vs §1256); from 2026 only **90 % of wagering losses** are deductible ([beancount tax guide](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/07/prediction-market-winnings-tax-kalshi-polymarket-guide), [TaxProf](https://taxprofblog.aals.org/2026/01/17/sports-gambling-and-the-taxation-of-prediction-markets)) [B]. Crypto-tax vendors cover Polymarket wallets ([Awaken](https://awaken.tax/integrations/polymarket-taxes), [CoinLedger](https://coinledger.io/blog/prediction-markets-tax)) [C]; nothing covers Kalshi/Novig/Polymarket US via API. The layer: import (Kalshi API fills, Polymarket US, Novig, CSV), FIFO/specific-ID ledger per contract, three-scenario output (other income / gambling with 90 % rule / 1256) with a one-page summary for the preparer, session logs for the gambling scenario. Who pays: traders $29–79/yr, CPAs white-label. Risk 1 (software, "not tax advice"). Reuses `oddsmath` + recorder code; belongs in `saas_portfolio`. Demand test: 20 posts/DMs in r/Kalshi, Kalshi Discord, two prediction-market newsletters in Jan–Feb 2027 window; pass = 10 pre-orders at $29.

## 6. Sex-work-adjacent legal businesses — out, with precision
* FOSTA (2018) created 18 U.S.C. §2421A (owning/operating a service "with the intent to promote or facilitate the prostitution of another person"); GAO-21-385 found it essentially unused for restitution as of Mar-2021; a Woodhull survey of 440 workers reported increased exposure to violence after screening/blacklist sites "shuttered overnight" ([Warren/Wyden SAFE SEX Workers Study Act release](https://www.warren.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/warren-lawmakers-introduce-bill-send-letter-urging-study-of-anti-sex-trafficking-legislations-impacts-on-sex-worker-health-safety)) [A/B]; academic review ([Anti-Trafficking Review](https://antitraffickingreview.org/index.php/atrjournal/article/download/448/364?inline=1)) [A].
* A bad-client registry or screening service used by workers outside Nevada's ~20 licensed brothels is, on its face, a §2421A "facilitation" risk regardless of intent; the only fully legal users (NV brothels) screen in-house. **Out.** Strip-club dancers and cam performers are legal workers: their wounds are misclassification/house fees (→ §8 wage lane) and payment access (→ #3), which we serve without touching sex work.

## 7. Gig / migrant / day labor — nonprofit lane

* The payer problem is absolute: workers cannot pay, employers will not, plaintiff firms already take the profitable slice, worker centers run on grants. The layer (hours/tip/house-fee log → claim packet, H-2A employer violation lookup from DOL enforcement data) is a good open-source tool for a worker center, not a venture. Keep as a **pro-bono agent project** if the owner wants a portfolio piece.

## 8. Reentry, tenant screening, timeshares, data brokers, consumer "scam-infested" lanes
* **Colorado Clean Slate (SB22-099)** (→ #6): automatic sealing since Jul-2024, >100k records expected; waits 4/7/10 years automatic vs 1–5 petition; CBI backlog ongoing; "more automated than automatic"; defendants often never learn; petition path still essential ([Colorado Lawyer](https://cl.cobar.org/features/automatic-record-sealing-in-colorado/), [Prison Legal News](https://www.prisonlegalnews.org/news/2025/mar/1/over-100000-criminal-records-sealed-under-colorados-clean-slate-act)) [A/B]. The known failure mode of every clean-slate law is **background-check vendors reporting sealed records** (FCRA §1681e(b)/§1681i duty). The layer: eligibility calculator from CO statute + petition packet generation (court forms are public) + "is my sealed record still showing?" checks + dispute-letter generation. Payer: legal-aid clinics, public defenders, reentry nonprofits (grants: Colorado Justice Reinvestment, Clean Slate Initiative); individuals $0–29. Not UPL if the tool fills court-published forms without individualized advice (same model as the courts' own self-help site). Score 16 → first alternate; revisit if a clinic will fund it.
* **Timeshares** (→ #7): 9.6M households own; ~9 % want out within two years (>850k people); 62 active exit companies; fees $4–18k; BBB: >$2.2M lost to 10 firms 2017–19; WA AG $2.62M settlement with Timeshare Exit Team ([Checkbook](https://www.checkbook.org/national/timeshare-exit-companies-deserve-a-time-out/articles/Trouble-with-Timeshare-Exit-Companies-7343)) [B]; Oregon REA resource ([Oregon](https://www.oregon.gov/rea/newsroom/Pages/2024-OREN-J/Timeshare-Owners-Exit-Resource.aspx)) [A]. Legit exits (developer deedback/"Responsible Exit" programs) are undocumented. A $99 router that tells an owner which resort program they qualify for, with the paperwork checklist and a blacklist, protects the participant; but Stripe prohibits "timeshare services" [A], several states regulate exit companies, and any document drafting edges toward UPL. Hold until #3 gives us a non-Stripe billing path; then test with 20 r/timeshare posters.
* **Data-broker opt-out:** DeleteMe/Optery/Incogni/Permission Slip + California's DROP (consumer registration from Jan-2026, broker processing from Aug-2026) → crowded and partly nationalizing; Colorado has no DROP. Skip.
* **Tenant screening (FCRA), junk fees, TCPA, auto-title/BHPH, crypto-recovery, immigration non-lawyer services, bail:** either UPL/CROA-adjacent (immigration, bail, credit/debt — Stripe prohibits debt relief and bail bonds), scam-infested with no clean payer (crypto recovery, TCPA serial litigation), or served by free nonprofits (Dollar For for medical debt) and CFPB-era vendors.
