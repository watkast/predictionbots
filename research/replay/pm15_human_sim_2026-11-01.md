# PM15 cockpit — human replay, 2026-11-01

**Question.** Is the cockpit's forecasting useful to a human who reads it and trades Polymarket US by hand?

**Method.** `scripts/pm15_human_sim.js` runs the cockpit page unmodified (DOM stub) against 224 recorded BTC 15-minute
rounds (Mr. P recorder, Oct 29 – Nov 2 2026: 1-Hz CF Benchmarks prints, the Polymarket US book top every second it
was seen, the exact price to beat and settlement — `mrp/mrp_extract_btc15.py`). The prints are fed to the page as its
spot feed (basis 0) and their 60-print average as the index series; the recorded book is its live book. Three personas
read only the DO NEXT instruction, tap after 8 s, and fill against the book at that second (best level, partial fills,
taker fee 0.0695·p·(1−p) both ways), one buy per round, $20 a trade:

- **lock** — buys only SURE THING, holds; sells when told SELL / SELL NOW
- **ef+lock** — also buys the Early Favorite instruction (favorite at 80–94¢ at 13:00 / 12:00 left)
- **row** — buys the favorite whenever the ask is at or under the next-trade price on the row (the GOOD price), plus the above

Each in two flavours: **obey** (sells at any bid on SELL) and **ev** (sells only when the bid beats what holding is worth).

## What it means

1. **LOCKED is right and unbuyable.** 224 of 224 locks held — the certainty logic is sound — but the first lock comes at a
   median 8 s before the bell, and in no round was the locked side offered with more than 10 s left. A human cannot buy
   a sure thing; the market has already withdrawn it. SURE THING is a HOLD signal, not a BUY signal.
2. **The late-buy trade does not exist at these prices.** Inside the last minute the eventual winner's ask (96–99¢) is
   above the page's calibrated chance for it (92–95%), and the book on the winner's side thins to a handful of seconds.
3. **The odds are now calibrated** (volatility from 5-s returns × 1.3): the page's 60–65% realizes 60%, 80–85% → 80%,
   90–95% → 93%, 95–100% → 98%. Before this replay the raw model was overconfident (90–95% realized 83%).
4. **The money is early, not late.** The only persona that trades — the row persona, buying the favorite when the ask is
   at or under the GOOD price and selling when the market overpays late — made +$215 (obey) / +$236 (ev) over 224
   rounds, positive on every full day, worst round −$12 on a $20 stake, 0 settlement losses. ≈ +5% of stake per round.
   The sell rules are net neutral in cash (≈ +$1,470 of losses avoided vs ≈ −$1,510 of winners given up); their value is
   the shape of the outcomes: no round lost the stake.
5. **The EF rule breaks even at best** (−$9 … +$3). Fees eat a favorite bought at 80–94¢ with a ~82% realized win rate.
6. **HOLD ≥ N s** holds the cushion 98.3% of the time against a 99% claim; 99.2% inside the final two minutes.
7. **SELL precision:** "SELL NOW — projected to lose" was right 48% of the time; "flip building" 16–30%. The sells are
   insurance, priced about fairly by the market, and the "ev" seller (sell only when the bid beats holding) is the better
   default for a human who can read a number before tapping. Speed matters: at 15 s latency the row persona's P&L drops
   from +$236 to +$144.

## Caveats

- The prints stand in for Coinbase, so the replay has no ~1 s lead and no bid-ask bounce: the live page should see flips
  slightly earlier than this. The records, the bad-minute ladder and the volatility multiplier were measured on the same
  four days (a mild look-ahead; the live page scans five days of Coinbase candles). Four volatile days, one regime.
- The book is seen 98% of seconds in the last three minutes, ~4% of seconds in the first five; early buys only happen
  when a snapshot existed within 20 s. Fills take the best level only; the fee is the taker fee both ways.
- 224 rounds is enough to kill the late-lock idea and to calibrate the odds; it is not enough to call +5% a round an
  edge. Run it paper, live, for two weeks — the page's own lock record and the paper bot are built for that.
