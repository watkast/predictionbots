# Mr. One: Mr. P's brain with resting limit orders — replayed, and why it is not an upgrade (Nov 2, 2026)

The idea (after a morning of Mr. E, the x/y price grid and the loser-bounce study): keep the brain that has the only edge we
have found — the calibrated chance from the settlement index, which leads the exchange price by seconds — and change the hands:
instead of Mr. P's taker buy inside a 15-s window and taker sells, keep ONE resting limit order on the book, re-priced every
second from the index. Bid on the favorite at (chance − 8¢) while flat; ask at (chance + 5¢) while holding; the page's risk
exits at the bid; LOCKED holds to the bell. No taker fee on entry, no race, nothing to chase. `scripts/pm15_live.js --maker`
(`scripts/mr1_live.js`) implements it; the replay's mock exchange fills a resting order only when the recorded book trades
through it (1¢ past the order, at the order's own price) and never lets a sale exceed what is held.

## The numbers

Same 224 recorded BTC 15-min rounds (Oct 29 – Nov 2), $20 cap, taker fees charged, books at most 1 s old (`--strict 1`;
the earlier harness let a quote be 20 s stale). P&L is cash at the end minus the $20 start.

| policy | trades | wins / losses | settle losses | worst | P&L |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mr. P as shipped** (taker buy after 2 s of discount, taker sells) | 186 | 137 / 49 | 0 | −$15.24 | **+$932** |
| Mr. One: resting bid may take the discount + 2-s persistence, ask only in the sell zone | 186 | 146 / 40 | 3 | −$15.27 | +$886 |
| Mr. One: resting bid may take the discount (no persistence), ask in the sell zone | 203 | 140 / 63 | 2 | −$10.98 | +$852 |
| Mr. One: same, but the ask rests all round at chance + 5¢ | 203 | 152 / 51 | 1 | −$10.86 | +$793 |
| Mr. One: pure maker (bid never crosses the ask) | 198 | 143 / 55 | 1 | −$16.94 | +$611 |
| Mr. One: pure maker, a touched order counts as filled (optimistic) | 204 | 147 / 57 | 2 | −$16.44 | +$663 |

With the old 20-s-stale books both look better (Mr. P +$1,034, Mr. One +$801) — stale quotes flatter a taker most.

## What the rounds say

Round by round (`one_x` vs `p_s1`): the pure-maker and all-round-ask variants lose the big rounds. Where Mr. P bought at
35–53¢ and sold at 89–99¢ (or held a LOCKED winner to the bell), the resting ask at chance + 5¢ was lifted at 41–60¢ — the
model's chance lags a running winner, so an ask at chance + edge sells the 99¢ rounds for a nickel. Restricting the ask to
the sell zone (last 5:00, as the page does) recovers most of that (+$793 → +$852). The resting bid loses on the other leg: a
bid that waits to be hit is hit on the way down (the adverse-selection result of the x/y grid again), and when the discount
does appear the taker gets the gap price while the maker gets its posted price. The 2-s persistence filter matters: without
it Mr. One buys first and worse (42¢ vs 35¢ two seconds later in the 21:45 round of Nov 1).

Three settlement losses vs Mr. P's zero: a resting ask can simply never be lifted, and the position goes to the bell; Mr. P's
taker harvest at the bid does not have that failure mode.

## Verdict

Not shipped as a bot. Every maker variant trails Mr. P; the best is Mr. P minus $46 with worse tail risk. What the test
confirms, from a different direction than the morning's price grid: in this market the edge is in *taking* a lagging quote
against the index, not in providing liquidity. The code stays (`--maker`, `--cross`, `--persist`, `--takeharvest`,
`--fillstrict`, `--strict`) as a tested harness for the next idea, the replay now charges taker fees and uses honest books,
and Mr. P gained one real fix from the exercise: it now cancels only its own order ids at start and stop (it used to cancel
every open order in the account — Mr. E's resting orders included).

Levers the data does support, not yet built: sizing by edge (Mr. P bets the full cap on every signal; the page already
computes an edge-scaled size), and running the same brain on ETH / SOL / XRP 15-min (same settlement rule, recorder data in
hand), which multiplies the number of discounted moments per day rather than squeezing more from each one.
