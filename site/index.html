What the terminal watches

Three kinds of moments where the rules have decided and the price has not caught up. Each one is small — tens to hundreds of contracts — which is why nobody with real size bothers, and why they exist.

Decided games

A first-half total already exceeded, a game already over, a market still quoting. Scores from the feeds, settlement rules from the venue, and a check that both venues agree before anything is flagged.

Kalshi MLS 1H total, 2 goals scored by the 40th minute, "over 1.5" still offered at 94¢.

The last seconds

Crypto Up/Down markets settle on a 60-second index average. With three seconds left the average has locked, and the winning side is still offered below $1 in roughly 4% of windows. Our recorder measured 168 of them in four days.

BTC 5-minute, 3 s left, running average 2.1 bps above the strike, "Up" offered at 73¢ × 384.

Cross-venue locks

The same contract on Polymarket US and Kalshi, priced apart by more than both fees. They last seconds; the scanner records every one, how long it lasted and how deep it was.

ETH 15-minute, Kalshi bid 0.999 for the leader, Polymarket offer 0.95 — 4.6¢ after fees, 5 seconds.
