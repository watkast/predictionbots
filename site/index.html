Kalshi (schedule of 7 July 2026): 7% × contracts × price × (1 − price), rounded up, on orders that take liquidity; most sports and economics series also charge resting orders 1.75%. Polymarket US (schedule of 6 November 2026): Θ × price × (1 − price) per share on orders that take liquidity, where Θ is 9.5% for accounts under $10,000 of 30-day taker volume, 8.5% to $50,000, 7.5% to $250,000 and 6.95% above; new accounts pay 6.95% for their first seven days; resting orders pay nothing. "No edge" means your win rate equals the price, which is what the market is telling you it is. Sources: the venues' published fee schedules, linked from our repo.

Here's the deal, if you still want to play

Both venues run new-player bonuses, and both pay publishers when a new account signs up through their links. Below is exactly what happens, in order. If you're trading money you need this month, close this tab.

Kalshi

Trade $25, get $25 in credit
  1. You open an account: name, address, date of birth, ID check. 18+, not in AZ, MN, NV or WA.
  2. You deposit (no minimum) and trade $25 in contracts within 90 days.
  3. Kalshi gives you $25 of trading credit. You cannot withdraw it; you have to trade it.
  4. Trading the $50 as 50¢ contracts costs about $1.75 in fees each way, before anyone wins or loses.
  5. We are not paid for this link yet. If Kalshi approves us as a partner, the rate appears here the day they tell us.

Polymarket US

  1. You open an account with a photo ID and a selfie. 18+, not in Nevada. A promo code cannot be added later.

  4. Fees on a 50¢ share: about 1.7¢ in your first week, then 2.4¢ (9.5% × price × (1 − price), the rate for everyone under $10,000 a month since 6 November 2026); resting orders are free.

Plain answers

Are you telling me not to trade?

We're telling you what it costs and how often people win. Most lose. If you still want to, the deal section shows exactly what happens, including whether we get paid.

You plan to take referral money. Why trust you?

Because the fees and the odds sit above every button, the rate we're paid is published, and the block disappears before the warning ever would. Members and data buyers pay us to keep it that way.

Did your own bots make money?

No. The ledger is the real record: we lost a little, learned three things, published all three, and stopped.

Which venues?

Polymarket US and Kalshi, the two US-regulated exchanges. Not the international Polymarket book.

Can I check your numbers?

Yes. Every post links its data file and script, every ledger row comes from a public log line, every fee figure follows the venue's schedule, and the code is public on GitHub.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is a record of what rules say, what markets showed, and what fees cost. You make your own decisions.
