"Is it safe" is two questions wearing one coat. Is my money safe from the company? Is my money safe from the market? Kalshi's answers are different, and most of what you will read online blurs them.

## Safe from the company: mostly, with limits

**Regulation.** Kalshi runs a designated contract market overseen by the Commodity Futures Trading Commission, the federal regulator of U.S. derivatives markets since 1974. Its clearinghouse, Kalshi Klear LLC, is separately registered with the CFTC as a derivatives clearing organization; the CFTC's current registration order for it is dated 15 April 2026. A related entity, Kalshi Prime LLC, is listed as a futures commission merchant. Kalshi's own site states the oversight plainly: "Kalshi is subject to U.S. regulatory oversight by the CFTC."

**Where the money sits.** Customer funds are held in segregated accounts through the clearinghouse with banking partners, apart from Kalshi's operating money, as federal derivatives rules and the clearinghouse's CFTC-filed rulebook require. Segregation is designed so that the company cannot use your balance and its creditors cannot reach it if the business fails.

**What that does not include.** There is no FDIC insurance on a Kalshi balance and no insurance fund behind it; Kalshi makes no claim otherwise. Segregation is a rule enforced by audits and regulators, not a guarantee fund, and the mechanics of unwinding a clearinghouse of this kind at scale have not been tested. Kalshi pays interest on balances from its banking partners (3.25% a year as of August 2026, variable, on balances of $250 or more); that is a pass-through, not deposit insurance.

**Getting your money out.** From Kalshi's help center: after a deposit, the deposited amount may be held before it can be withdrawn, timed from the deposit itself. Debit card, Apple Pay, Google Pay and ACH deposits can be withdrawn to the same method once the deposit settles, and to a different method up to two days after settlement. PayPal, Venmo, crypto and wire deposits carry no hold. Holds apply only to the deposited amount; winnings can be withdrawn once they are settled cash, and money in open positions cannot be withdrawn until those positions close. Crypto withdrawal requires a prior crypto deposit.

**Account security.** Use a unique password and turn on two-factor authentication in your account settings; no venue can protect an account whose email has been taken over.

## Safe from the law: contested, and that matters for sports markets

Kalshi's event contracts on sports are the subject of an open fight between the federal regulator, which oversees Kalshi, and states that regulate gambling. On 23 April 2026 Wisconsin's attorney general sued Kalshi, Polymarket, Robinhood, Coinbase and Crypto.com, asking a court to declare that offering sports event contracts to Wisconsin residents violates state gambling law and to stop it. The state said it is targeting companies, not users, and sports, not politics or world-events markets. Other states have issued warnings or brought actions over sports contracts, and Kalshi's own new-customer promotion excludes Arizona, Minnesota, Nevada and Washington.

What that means for a user: a market you hold a position in could be closed or geofenced in your state by a court or a regulator; your funds remain yours under the segregation rules above, but the markets available to you can change with little notice.

## Safe from other traders: the rules are new and the enforcement has started

In April 2026 the Department of Justice and the CFTC brought a criminal and a civil case over insider trading on prediction markets, and Kalshi suspended three political candidates for trading on their own races under its Rule 5.17(z). The platforms now run surveillance and ban participants who can influence outcomes. [How it works and how it gets caught.](/learn/insider-trading-on-prediction-markets.html) The person on the other side of an insider's trade is an ordinary account; the protection is after the fact.

## Safe from the market: no, and this is the part that costs people money

None of the above changes the arithmetic. Kalshi charges 7% × contracts × price × (1 − price) on orders that take liquidity, and 1.75% on resting orders in most sports and economics series. A two-sided market returns your stake minus those fees on average. Most participants lose, and the ones who lose most are the ones who trade most. **Bottom line.** Your deposit on Kalshi is about as protected from the company as money on a U.S. derivatives exchange gets: regulated, segregated, uninsured. It is not protected from the fee, from a state closing a market you are in, or from someone who knows more than you. "Safe" was never the question that decides whether you come out ahead.
