Most of what circulates about prediction markets is anecdote: a screenshot of a win, a thread about a whale. Below are the numbers that come from measurement, each with the study it came from and the date we last read it.

Why most lose is arithmetic, not character: a two-sided market returns the stake minus fees on average, and the fees on Kalshi and Polymarket US run 3.5% to 4.8% of a 50¢ trade for an ordinary account ([Kalshi](/learn/kalshi-fees.html), [Polymarket US](/learn/polymarket-us-fees.html)). Whoever quotes both sides collects; whoever clicks pays.

## What legal online betting did to household finances

The largest study to date followed the credit records of 4.38 million U.S. adults from March 2016 to June 2023 across states that did and did not legalize sports betting. It measured whole-state populations, not just bettors, so the effects below are diluted by everyone who never placed a bet.

| In states that legalized **online** sports betting | Change within about four years |
| --- | --- |
| Likelihood of a personal bankruptcy filing | **+25% to +30%** |
| Debt in collections | **+8%** |
| Credit card delinquencies | **+8%** |
| Auto loan delinquencies | **+8%** |
| Credit scores | **about −2%** |
| States with in-person betting only | effects very small or not statistically significant |

Source: Brett Hollenbeck (UCLA Anderson), Poet Larsen and Davide Proserpio (USC), "The Financial Consequences of Legalized Sports Gambling," SSRN working paper 4903302, 2024, using the University of California Credit Panel. The authors suggest the strongest effects fall on people from their late twenties to mid-thirties. The online channel, not betting itself, carried the damage, which is the channel every prediction market uses.

Prediction markets are not sports betting in law, and no study of this size exists for them yet; they are, however, online, always open, and settle in minutes, which are the properties this study found did the harm.

## How many people this is

The National Council on Problem Gambling estimates that in any given year about **2.5 million U.S. adults** meet the criteria for a severe gambling problem and a further **5 to 8 million** have mild or moderate problems. Most never contact anyone about it. If the app has become hard to put down, you are not an outlier; you are in a group the size of a large city, and the line is free: **1-800-GAMBLER**, call or text, 24 hours, or [help in your state](https://www.ncpgambling.org/help-treatment/help-by-state/).
